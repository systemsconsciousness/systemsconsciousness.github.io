    The Intersection of Ancient Zen & Cybernetic Thought

    Why combine Zen Buddhism, Systems Dynamics, and Alex Kidd in Miracle World? Because Jan-Ken-Pon (Rock, Paper, Scissors) is the ultimate cyclical feedback loop: no state is absolute, and victory lies in understanding the whole dynamic cycle.

    Codex Knowledge Base

    The Philosophy

    Systems Thinking gives us the formal language of stocks, flows, balancing loops, reinforcing cascades, delays, and emergence. It allows us to analyze how economies, ecologies, organizations, and networks behave over time.

    Zen Awareness gives us the direct experiential realization of non-duality (advaita / shunya) — loosening attachment to rigid mental models, embracing uncertainty, and acting with effortless responsiveness (wu wei).

    The Jan-Ken-Pon Metaphor

    In Miracle World, boss battles are decided not by brute force, but by Jan-Ken-Pon (Rock Paper Scissors). Rock crushes Scissors, Scissors cuts Paper, Paper covers Rock. This transitive cycle is the core axiom of cybernetics: hierarchical domination is an illusion in any closed loop.
